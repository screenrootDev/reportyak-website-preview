    What Are Sustainability Reporting Standards?

    Firms need common rules to earn trust. Sustainability reporting standards define those rules in plain language. A sustainability reporting standard sets what to measure and share. It guides format, metrics, and deadlines for non-financial reporting. The result is data audiences can read, compare, and believe.

    Clear standards turn scattered numbers into comparable ESG pictures. They align firms with investor reporting requirements and public expectations. Regulators and the accounting standards board then check data against one yardstick. That consistency lets markets weigh risks and opportunities without guesswork. It also cuts audit time and lowers compliance costs every year.

    No single group controls the field; many voices shape guidelines. The value reporting foundation, reporting council, and GRI each issue rules. AT&T prepared its fiscal-year 2024 report in line with the GRI Standards and a detailed index online. These overlapping sustainability reporting standards let firms choose what suits best.     Key Frameworks Every Company Should Know

    Below are key sustainability reporting standards every company should know for 2025.

    GRI Standards – Broad, impact-based

    • Adopted by over ten thousand organizations worldwide. The GRI standards give a public lens on social, environmental, and economic impacts. They give modular guidance and added Biodiversity and Mining topics in 2024. These formally apply to reporting years starting on or after January 1, 2026 and help firms disclose broad sustainability dimensions in plain language.

    ISSB / IFRS S1 & S2 – Investor-focused

    • Listed companies seeking capital efficiency follow the international sustainability standards board global baseline. Its S1 and S2 standards take effect on 1 January 2024. They link ESG metrics to financials, giving one clear view of risks, opportunities, and value for smoother global reporting.

    SASB – Sector specific 

    • Manufacturers, banks, and tech giants use SASB Standards, created by the sustainability accounting standards board. These rules spotlight ESG issues that matter for each industry. The ISSB will update them in 2024-26, but the sector metrics stay optional. Firms still rely on them to guide investor talks worldwide.

    TCFD (moving into ISSB) IFRS

    • The Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures ends in 2024. Its guidance moves into the IFRS sustainability disclosure standards. Firms used to TCFD pillars will see nearly the same climate details in IFRS S2. The switch stays smooth and keeps investors focused on scenarios and governance.

    CDP – Environmental disclosure 

    • More than 23,000 firms fill CDP’s yearly survey. It tracks water, forests, and climate work. The 2024 survey aligns with IFRS S2, so companies meet investor data needs fast. It also covers extra environmental topics many frameworks label as additional standards.

    CSRD & ESRS – EU rules AMC’s Group

    • Large EU firms must follow the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive. New European rules start with 2024 reports filed in 2025. Smaller companies join in 2026-28. Sector-specific additional standards will come later. This timeline secures uniform double-materiality disclosures across supply chains.

    BRSR – India’s mandatory ESG format AMC’s Group

    • Since 2023, India’s Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report covers the top 1,000 listed firms. They answer 140 indicators and add supply-chain data from 2025-26. This specific sustainability reporting format aligns with GRI and TCFD. Investors see familiar data, and firms skip foreign legal codes.
